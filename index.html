Why Single-Layer CSS Box Shadows Look Muddy

        
A default single-declaration box-shadow: 0 12px 24px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.25) distributes blur uniformly across one bounding box, creating a harsh grey halo around cards and modals. In physical lighting environments, ambient occlusion produces a tight, dark contact shadow directly underneath an elevated surface alongside a wide, low-opacity atmospheric penumbra.

        
Stacking 3 to 6 comma-separated shadow layers with exponentially doubling vertical offsets (2px, 4px, 8px, 16px, 32px) and matching blur radii replicates natural light attenuation while keeping total alpha balanced so dark and light UI themes both remain crisp.

      

      

        
GPU Compositing & Animation Performance Tips

        
Animating box-shadow directly inside CSS @keyframes forces the browser to repaint the element's layer on every frame. To maintain locked 60fps or 120fps hover transitions, render the layered shadow on a ::after pseudo-element and transition only its opacity and transform properties, which execute strictly on the GPU compositor thread.

      

      

        
Frequently Asked Questions

        

          

            
How many box-shadow layers should I use in production?

            
Three to four layers deliver smooth optical depth for standard cards and buttons without measurable paint overhead, while five to six layers work best for large hero modals and floating popovers.

          

          

            
How do I use multi-layer shadows in Tailwind CSS?

            
Paste the generated shadow-[...] arbitrary utility class directly onto any Tailwind element, or register the custom value under theme.extend.boxShadow in your Tailwind configuration.
